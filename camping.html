Favorite Michigan Campgrounds

The state parks we actually go back to — and the specific sites we book when we do.

Every campground listing online tells you the same things: number of sites, whether there's electric, how far to the beach. None of them tell you which site to book.

These are the parks we keep coming back to, with the exact site numbers we've stayed on, how they drain after a heavy rain, and how they work with a toddler and an infant along. That's the part we always wished somebody had written down.

Roscommon County

North Higgins Lake State Park

Very family friendly, with a great beach and a lot quieter than South Higgins. There's a boat ramp and plenty of places to walk. Our toddler loved how much room he had to ride his bike.

Last visit was June 2026 and we can't wait to get back.

Our Favorite Sites
East Loop — 136, 11, 20, 32

Very large area for our tent setup and fairly flat. Little to no standing water when it rains.

Cheboygan County

Burt Lake State Park

Very open and large park with an amazing beach right on Burt Lake, lots of water toy access, and connections to the surrounding lakes. Two different playgrounds and plenty of room for kids to run.

We did the gender reveal for our youngest here on Burt Lake beach and had a lot of people stop to congratulate us.

Our Favorite Sites
302, 303, 267 & 210

All fairly large flat dirt sites where little to no water gathers in rain. Worth knowing: 302 and 303 have vault toilets right behind them, so during busy weeks you may get campers cutting around the site.

St. Ignace

Straits State Park

Our number one favorite site anywhere is here. You can hear the waves from the beach, and it's still an electric site.

You do hear foot traffic from people heading to the beach, but nothing ever too crazy.

Our Favorite Sites
116B is the favorite — also 72, 73, 74 & 187

116B is the more secluded one. 187 is the largest site we've stayed on at Straits — very flat and grassy, could genuinely fit more than one tent, or a camper and a tent together.

Lenawee County

Hayes State Park

We've stayed here quite a few times because it's close — within twenty minutes of both home and work, which makes it an easy summer weekend getaway.

Better in spring and summer for camping. There's a boat launch, and a separate state park around the corner with another launch, a playground and a beach. Plenty of walking trails, nothing too technical.

Waterloo Recreation Area

Portage Lake

Very good beach plus an amazing playground, and the beach has a concession stand. There's a boat ramp too. Our son loves going for walks down the trails here.

Our Favorite Sites
122, 124, 53, 74, 76 — and 114 for seclusion

All larger sites, a mix of dirt and grass, great for tenting. 122 is the largest we've stayed in at Portage and 114 is the most secluded. Some sites pool a little water in heavy rain, but nothing crazy.

Southern Michigan

Sugarloaf State Park

Another of our favorite close-to-home parks — we'll go even just for a weekend. One of the better playgrounds for the kids and a very well kept beach.

Our Favorite Sites
S83, S85, S88, S151 & S150

S83 and S85 are open sun with no shade but full grass, no dirt. S151 and S150 are very large corner sites and feel a lot more welcoming.

Augusta, MI

Fort Custer Recreation Area

Miles and miles of walking and biking trails. I remember going out with a stroller and our oldest and covering at least seven miles, finding all the little ponds and lakes tucked back in there. Great fishing around them.

Our Favorite Sites
111, 112, 174, 175, 177 & 157

All fairly flat with a mix of grass and dirt. From what we've seen there's nowhere that holds standing water after a heavy rain.

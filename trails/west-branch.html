About the Area

The West Branch, Ogemaw Hills, and St. Helen area is one of those places where you can ride it multiple times and have a completely different experience depending on the season and weather.

There's a lot of sandy trail, forest roads, two-tracks, mud holes, and low areas that collect water. When everything is dry, a lot of the riding is pretty manageable. Add rain, spring thaw, or snow and things can change quickly.

That's one of the things I like about this area.

It's not necessarily the most technical place we ride, but the weather can turn an easy trail into a really fun day of wheeling.

Trail Conditions

Expect:

  • Sand
  • Loose dirt
  • Forest two-tracks
  • Mud holes
  • Standing water
  • Ruts
  • Roots
  • Seasonal roads
  • Snow during winter rides

Some areas can also become extremely wet or temporarily impassable after major storms, so I always recommend checking current trail conditions before heading out.

Solo or Buddy?

Solo or Buddy?

My recommendation: Solo is possible, buddy recommended.

There are definitely parts of the West Branch area where I would be comfortable riding alone, especially if I know the route and conditions are dry.

But this area can fool you.

A normal-looking mud hole can be much deeper than expected, and snow or heavy rain can completely change a trail you have driven plenty of times before.

For casual exploring, solo can be fine if you're prepared. If we're actually going out to wheel, especially during spring, winter, or after heavy rain, I would rather have another vehicle with me.

Permits

Permits

If we're heading out specifically to use the ORV trail system, I recommend buying the Michigan ORV License and ORV Trail Permit.

Make sure you're also paying attention to trail width designations. Some trails in this area are intended for 50-inch machines, while designated ORV Routes are where full-size vehicles belong.

Always check current Michigan DNR trail maps, closures, and rules before your trip.

My Advice

West Branch is a great area if you're newer to wheeling because you can usually find bypasses and easier routes.

It's also one of my favorite areas for winter riding.

Snow completely changes these trails. Sections you've driven dozens of times can suddenly require momentum, recovery gear, and a completely different line.

If you're bringing the family, this is an easy place to build a shorter half-day trip or turn it into an all-day adventure.

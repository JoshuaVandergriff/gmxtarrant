# St. Ignace Area

**Overall Rating:** 4.5/5  
**Difficulty:** Easy to Moderate  
**Family Friendly:** 5/5  
**Solo or Buddy:** Completely doable solo, but a buddy is recommended when exploring questionable water crossings or unfamiliar areas.

## Quick Take

Our family favorite. We've traveled well over 500 miles of trails, forest roads, and two-tracks throughout the St. Ignace area, and it is still the place we look forward to getting back to the most.

## About the Area

The St. Ignace area is easily one of our favorite places to explore. Over the years, we've traveled well over 500 miles of trails, forest roads, and two-tracks throughout this area, and there is still always somewhere new to check out.

As a family, this is by far our favorite place to be.

There is something about the eastern U.P. that keeps bringing us back. You can spend an entire day exploring without needing every mile of the ride to be technical. There are forest roads, sandy two-tracks, muddy sections, ORV routes, and long stretches where you're simply out in the middle of nowhere enjoying the scenery.

This is also a great area if you're bringing the family or if you have a mostly stock Bronco or other capable four-wheel-drive vehicle. Most of the riding isn't extremely difficult, but that doesn't mean there aren't areas that can catch you off guard. Depending on the weather, you'll find mud, standing water, washed-out sections, and rougher two-tracks that can make the day a lot more interesting.

One of the things I really like about St. Ignace is that the ride actually feels like an adventure. You aren't just driving in circles around the same trail system. You can cover a ton of ground, travel between completely different areas, and build a route that takes you through the woods, small towns, and remote parts of the eastern U.P.

## Trail Conditions

Expect a mixture of:

- Forest two-tracks
- Hard-packed dirt
- Gravel
- Sand
- Mud
- Standing water
- Water holes after heavy rain
- Occasional rocky sections
- Washed-out or rutted two-tracks

Dry weather makes a lot of this area pretty manageable, but conditions can change fast after rain.

There are also some low-lying areas that hold a lot of water, and depending on recent weather, a trail that was easy on your last trip may look completely different the next time through.

That's part of what keeps the area interesting even after we've already put hundreds of miles on these trails.

## Solo or Buddy?

**My recommendation: Completely doable solo, but know when to use your judgment.**

St. Ignace is absolutely an area I would be comfortable exploring solo.

We've spent enough time throughout the area that there are plenty of routes I wouldn't hesitate to run with just our vehicle and the family.

That being said, there are definitely some sketchy areas that can get a lot of water. Some of the holes and flooded sections can be difficult to judge from inside the vehicle, especially if you don't know how deep they are or what the bottom looks like.

If you're looking at something and you're not sure about it, that's where having another vehicle with you becomes a really good idea.

There's nothing wrong with turning around, finding another route, or waiting until you have someone else with you before testing a questionable water hole.

So while I definitely don't consider a buddy vehicle required around St. Ignace, if you're unfamiliar with the area, heading deeper into the woods, or planning to explore after a lot of rain, having another rig with you is always the safer option.

## Permits

If you're planning on using Michigan's designated ORV trail system, I recommend getting both the **Michigan ORV License and ORV Trail Permit**.

Even though a street-licensed vehicle can legally use some ORV routes without them under certain circumstances, we buy the permits when we're going out specifically to wheel. They're inexpensive, support the trail system, and eliminate the guessing about which sections require what.

Always check current Michigan DNR rules before your trip.

## My Advice

If you're new to trail riding or you're taking younger kids, St. Ignace is an awesome place to start.

Bring basic recovery gear, have a tire repair kit and compressor, download your maps before leaving pavement, and make sure you have enough fuel before heading deep into some of the more remote areas.

Don't judge water holes by what you can see from the driver's seat. If you're unsure, get out and look at it before driving in.

This isn't necessarily where we go looking for the biggest obstacles.

This is where we go when we want to load up the family, disappear into the woods for the day, and explore.

After more than 500 miles of trail riding around St. Ignace and the eastern U.P., it is still the place our family looks forward to going back to the most.

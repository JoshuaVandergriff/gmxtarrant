# Drummond Island

**Overall Rating:** 5/5  
**Difficulty:** Moderate to Difficult  
**Family Friendly:** 4.5/5  
**Solo or Buddy:** Buddy required in my opinion for serious trail riding.

## Quick Take

One of Michigan's best true off-road destinations. Rocks, ledges, mud, water, and technical trails make this one worth the ferry ride.

## About the Area

If someone asked me where to go in Michigan for a true off-road adventure, Drummond Island would be near the top of my list every time.

Drummond is different from most of the trail riding we do in Michigan. You're dealing with limestone rock, ledges, mud, water, tight wooded sections, and terrain where picking the right line actually matters.

There are easier sections of the island, so you don't need a completely built rig to enjoy Drummond, but there are absolutely places where lockers, good tires, ground clearance, and a spotter make a huge difference.

And then there's Marble Head.

That's one of those places every Michigan off-roader should experience at least once. The ride out there gets progressively rougher and gives you some legitimate rock and ledge obstacles before eventually reaching one of the coolest viewpoints on the island.

## Trail Conditions

Drummond can throw a little bit of everything at you:

- Limestone rock
- Rock shelves and ledges
- Mud
- Standing water
- Tight wooded trails
- Roots
- Rough two-tracks
- Deep holes
- Slick rock when wet

Weather makes a huge difference here. Something that isn't a big deal during a dry weekend can become a completely different obstacle after heavy rain.

## Solo or Buddy?

**My recommendation: Buddy required.**

Personally, Drummond is not somewhere I recommend going deep into the trail system alone.

There are sections where you can break something, get stuck, or need a spotter, and you're a long way from an easy recovery. Some obstacles also look completely different from behind the wheel than they do from outside the vehicle.

For a serious Drummond ride, I want at least two vehicles, and three or more is even better.

If you're only cruising some of the easier roads around the island, that's different. But if you're heading toward Marble Head or actually planning to wheel, bring a buddy.

## Permits

For the state ORV trail system, I recommend having the **Michigan ORV License and ORV Trail Permit**.

Full-size vehicles need to stay on routes designated for full-size ORVs. There are ATV-only trails on the island that a Bronco, Jeep, or truck should not be on.

There are also privately operated off-road areas on Drummond Island that may have their own admission fees and rules separate from the Michigan DNR trail system.

And don't forget one important part of the trip: you're taking the ferry over from DeTour Village, so plan fuel, food, and timing accordingly.

Always check current Michigan DNR and local rules before your trip.

## My Advice

Bring real recovery gear here.

At minimum, I want a kinetic rope or recovery strap, soft shackles, tire repair kit, compressor, basic tools, and offline maps. If your rig has a winch, even better.

Drummond feels like you're actually going somewhere and accomplishing something instead of just driving down a dirt road.

It's one of my favorite places we've wheeled in Michigan.

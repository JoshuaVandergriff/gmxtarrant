GPX FILES — READ THIS BEFORE YOU UPLOAD ANY
============================================

Put your route files in this folder with these EXACT names:

    st-ignace.gpx
    drummond-island.gpx
    west-branch.gpx
    silver-lake-sand-dunes.gpx

Lowercase, hyphens, no spaces. The download buttons on the trail pages
are already pointing at these names, so the moment a file lands here it works.


⚠️  THE ONE THING YOU MUST DO FIRST — TRIM THE TRACK
-----------------------------------------------------

If you hit "record" in your driveway on Moon Lake Rd and stopped recording
when you got home, that GPX file contains your home address. Anyone who
downloads it can see exactly where you live, and they can see it in a
program that puts a pin on a map.

This is the single biggest risk in publishing GPX files and it is easy to miss
because the file looks like a bunch of numbers.

BEFORE you export any track:

  1. Delete every trackpoint from your house to the trailhead
  2. Delete every trackpoint from the trailhead back home
  3. Start the published track AT the trailhead or staging area
  4. End it AT the trailhead
  5. Open the trimmed file in Google Earth (free) and look at both ends
     before you publish. If either end is a house, trim more.

Same goes for anywhere else you don't want pinned — a friend's property,
a camp spot you want to keep quiet, a hunting spot.


HOW TO TRIM
-----------

Easiest: whatever app recorded it usually lets you crop or split a track
before exporting. Look for "trim," "crop," "split," or "edit track."

Free desktop option: GPX Editor, or the free tier of gpx.studio (in your
browser — open the file, drag the start and end points, export).

Google Earth Pro (free) will open a GPX and show you the whole track on a
map, which is the fastest way to verify you trimmed enough.


A FEW MORE THINGS
-----------------

- Don't publish tracks that cross private property, closed trails, or
  anywhere you weren't supposed to be. It's your name on the file.

- Keep files reasonably small. A full day of one-second recording can be
  several megabytes. Most apps can reduce or simplify trackpoints before
  export — 5 to 10 second intervals is plenty and keeps files under 1 MB.

- Name the track inside the file too, not just the filename. Most apps let
  you set a track name. "GMxTARRANT — St. Ignace Area" shows up in whatever
  program opens it, which is free advertising every time it's shared.

- Keep the raw untrimmed originals somewhere OUTSIDE this folder. Everything
  in the gmxtarrant-site folder gets uploaded to the live website.
